# MenoLisa Lower Body Strength Program
### An 8-Week Progressive Plan for Women 45+

---

## Program Philosophy

Same logic as the upper-body plan: nothing new gets introduced mid-program —
every movement pattern is already "in the room" from Week 1, just at an
easier regression. The squat, step, and lunge families each have a built-in
ladder in your recorded library, so the plan gets harder by changing *which
variant* plays a role rather than by teaching a brand-new pattern every
few weeks. That matters more for lower body than upper body — balance and
single-leg control take longer to earn trust in this age group than upper
body pressing does, so nothing jumps in difficulty without an unloaded
version coming first.

**Frequency:** 2x/week (non-consecutive days), or 3x/week if recovery allows.
**Rest:** 60–90 sec between accessory sets, 90–120 sec between structural sets
(Goblet Squat, Sumo Deadlift).
**Load cue:** "Heavy" means the last 2 reps of a set feel genuinely hard with
clean form — not a number on the dumbbell. Re-test loadable weight every 2 weeks.
**Balance note:** every single-leg exercise (step-ups, split squats, lunges)
can be done near a wall or countertop for light fingertip support — this
doesn't make it "not count." Building confidence on one leg is the actual
goal at this age, not just the muscle.

| Block | Goal | What fills it here |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Prep | Wake up the nervous system, groove the squat pattern | Bodyweight squat family (rotates by phase) |
| 2. Structural | Bone density + foundational lower-body power | Goblet Squat + Dumbbell Sumo Deadlift |
| 3. Functional | Balance, single-leg strength, multi-directional stability | Step-Up + Lunge/Split Squat family |
| 4. Accessory | Protect ankles/knees, build lower-leg and balance resilience | Calf Raise progression |

---

## Phase 1 · Weeks 1–2 — Foundation
*Goal: groove the squat pattern, build single-leg confidence at the easiest entry point for each family.*

**Block 1 — Prep**
- Chair Squat — 2 sets x 10 reps *(sit back to a light touch on the chair — teaches hip-hinge depth safely)*

**Block 2 — Structural (Heavy Load)**
- Goblet Squat — 3 sets x 10 reps
- Dumbbell Sumo Deadlift — 3 sets x 10 reps *(light-to-moderate load — this phase is about grooving the hip-hinge pattern)*

**Block 3 — Functional (Balance & Single-Leg)**
- Step-Up (bodyweight) — 2 sets x 8 reps/side
- Supported Reverse Lunge — 2 sets x 8 reps/side *(hand on a wall/counter — easiest lunge entry, minimal balance demand)*

**Block 4 — Accessory / Joint Protection**
- Calf Raise — 2 sets x 12 reps

---

## Phase 2 · Weeks 3–4 — Build
*Goal: add load to the step pattern, move the lunge from supported to dynamic/unassisted.*

**Block 1 — Prep**
- Bodyweight Squat — 2 sets x 10 reps

**Block 2 — Structural (Heavy Load)**
- Goblet Squat — 3 sets x 8 reps *(increase load, drop reps slightly)*
- Dumbbell Sumo Deadlift — 3 sets x 8 reps, heavier

**Block 3 — Functional (Balance & Single-Leg)**
- Step-Up, loaded — 3 sets x 8 reps/side
- Walking Lunge — 3 sets x 10 reps/side *(no wall support now — dynamic, moving through space)*

**Block 4 — Accessory / Joint Protection**
- Calf Raise — 3 sets x 12 reps

---

## Phase 3 · Weeks 5–6 — Progress
*Goal: introduce the static single-leg squat patterns (unloaded first) and add external load to the calves.*

**Block 1 — Prep**
- Prisoner Squat — 2 sets x 10 reps *(hands behind head — removes arm counterbalance, asks the core to control the descent)*

**Block 2 — Structural (Heavy Load)**
- Goblet Squat — 4 sets x 8 reps, heavier
- Dumbbell Sumo Deadlift — 4 sets x 8 reps, heavier

**Block 3 — Functional (Balance & Single-Leg)**
- Split Squat — 3 sets x 8 reps/side *(bodyweight — front foot stays put, this is the entry point before Bulgarian)*
- Bulgarian Split Squat — 2 sets x 6 reps/side *(bodyweight — rear foot elevated on a chair/step, hold something for balance the first week)*

**Block 4 — Accessory / Joint Protection**
- Calf Raise, loaded — 3 sets x 12 reps

---

## Phase 4 · Weeks 7–8 — Peak
*Goal: heaviest structural load of the program; single-leg patterns graduate to fully loaded.*

**Block 1 — Prep**
- Air Squat — 2 sets x 12 reps *(quick, rhythmic bodyweight squats — primes the nervous system before heavy loading)*

**Block 2 — Structural (Heavy Load)**
- Goblet Squat — 4 sets x 6 reps, heaviest load of the program
- Dumbbell Sumo Deadlift — 4 sets x 6 reps, heaviest load of the program

**Block 3 — Functional (Balance & Single-Leg)**
- Split Squat, loaded — 3 sets x 8 reps/side
- Bulgarian Split Squat, loaded — 3 sets x 8 reps/side *(the program's hardest single-leg movement — full external load, rear foot elevated)*

**Block 4 — Accessory / Joint Protection**
- Calf Raise, loaded — 3 sets x 15 reps

---

## Progression Logic at a Glance

| Exercise | Wk 1–2 | Wk 3–4 | Wk 5–6 | Wk 7–8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Block 1 Prep (rotates) | Chair Squat | Bodyweight Squat | Prisoner Squat | Air Squat |
| Goblet Squat (Block 2) | 3x10 | 3x8 | 4x8 | 4x6 |
| Sumo Deadlift (Block 2) | 3x10 | 3x8 | 4x8 | 4x6 |
| Step pattern (Block 3) | Step-Up 2x8/side | Step-Up loaded 3x8/side | *(carried at loaded volume)* | *(carried at loaded volume)* |
| Lunge/Split pattern (Block 3) | Supported Reverse Lunge 2x8/side | Walking Lunge 3x10/side | Split Squat 3x8/side + Bulgarian (bw) 2x6/side | Split Squat loaded 3x8/side + Bulgarian loaded 3x8/side |
| Calf Raise (Block 4) | 2x12 | 3x12 | Loaded 3x12 | Loaded 3x15 |

**Why this order:** the squat pattern is grooved unloaded in Block 1 every
single session before it's asked to bear heavy load in Block 2 — this is the
same "pattern before load" logic as the upper-body plan's push-up ladder.
Block 3 never jumps straight to a loaded single-leg movement — each family
(step, lunge/split) gets one full phase unloaded or supported before load or
elevation gets added, which is the safest way to build real single-leg trust
rather than just single-leg fatigue.

## Notes for App Implementation
- Chair Squat and Prisoner Squat both work well as "reset" options if a user needs to repeat Phase 1 or Phase 3 before progressing — flag them in-app as regression options on the Goblet Squat and Split Squat cards respectively.
- The Bulgarian Split Squat (Phase 3–4) is the single hardest movement in the plan — worth a dedicated form-cue video (rear foot placement, torso lean, knee tracking) since it's the one most likely to cause hesitation in this age group.
- Same phase-based asset reuse logic as the upper-body plan applies here: video/demo assets can be grouped by phase since only load/rep-count text changes within most exercise families.
